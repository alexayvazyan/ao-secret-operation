Method (1): likely inversion vs. likely reading

Find AO forward passes that probably inverted vs. probably read, then compare the AO's internals between the groups

Core question
Is there a mechanistic fingerprint inside the AO that separates
text inversion from reading?
Step 1 · build two groups from arithmetic prompts
Label AO passes by which route they probably took
Grouping
Likely text inversion
The target model answers the problem wrong, but the AO reading its activations answers it right. The right answer is not in the model's computation, so the AO probably solved the text itself.
Likely reading
The activation stream is missing a key piece of context needed to solve the problem from text, so a correct AO answer has to come from what the model computed.
Problem: candidates were thin, and an "AO right, target wrong" case may just be an unlucky slip by the target, not inversion.
Step 2 · compare activation vectors
Cosine similarity between groups and against direct computation
Uninformative

Similarities were very high both with direct computation and between the groups.

Similar activation vectors can still produce substantially different logits, so high similarity says little about which route was taken.

Step 3 · attribute the answer logit
Direct logit attribution per attention head and MLP layer
Failed to generalize

Contrasting per-component contributions between the groups identified several candidate heads.

The heads did not generalize across problem sets or to non-arithmetic examples.

What this motivated
Groups defined by guessing the route are too noisy to fingerprint. The next methods instead build settings where the route is known: compare against the AO solving the text directly (Method 2), or give the target model thoughts that disagree with the text, so that only reading can produce the right answer (Method 3).
